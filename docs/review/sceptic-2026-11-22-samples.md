# Sceptic review on sample sizes and representativeness, 2026-11-22

Asked by the owner on 2026-11-21: the gate's test set "should have fights throughout, a representative sample"; "I wonder if our samples across the board are too small"; "run a skeptic cycle on sample sizes and representativeness given the complexity and specifics of this game". Written by the orchestrator from the primary files (the gate design `backlog/act3-week/GATE-V5.md` and its measured spreads, the whole-run judge's `promotion.json` files, the real block `2026-11-20T1904`, the generation-20 plain-run store, lane 5's Act 3 exam, the 11-21 sceptic review), with the arithmetic in `scratchpad/samples_power.py` and `mark_odds.py` (reproduced in section 1) and the data facts gathered by a measuring worker (`backlog/act3-week/sample-sceptic/facts.md`, 0.1 CPU-hours; section 6 lists what it changed). Every margin below is two standard errors (the 95 in 100 range) unless it says otherwise. "SE" is one standard error.

## Verdict in five lines

1. **The samples are the right size for the big questions and too small for the small ones we have started asking of them.** A 50-point gap to humans at the Act 3 fights, a champion that gains 2 to 3 passes per 100 runs across several promotions, a 4-point effect of thinking longer: every sample we run sees these. A single fight promotion's effect on whole runs (about half a pass per 100 runs) is seen by nothing we run: at 4,000 pairs the whole-run judge resolves floors (SE 0.19) and nothing else, and its Act 3 and win figures for one candidate (+0.53 per 100 runs, SE 0.5; 11 against 13 wins) have been quoted as readings when they are noise.
2. **The weekly real block cannot see a week's progress.** 2,000 runs give about 350 arrivals at the first Act 3 boss (100 of them finished by the referee's stand-in); one block reads to about plus or minus 4 per 100 arrivals, two blocks compared to about 6. The 2 December mark (23, today 17.5) is 5.5 away: a player that truly reached 23 passes the block one time in two, one at 25 four times in five, one that did not move 3 times in 1,000. The mark is strict against a false pass and a coin flip at the mark itself.
3. **The gate's test set is representative of where runs die in Acts 1 and 2 and at the Act 3 bosses, and misses Act 3's ordinary and elite fights**, where 12.6 of every 100 real runs die, 13 in 100 of all deaths, more than at any single boss. The omission is a choice in two builder files, not a limit of the data: the human record holds every Act 3 floor and our own store holds 4,115 Act 3 hallway fights and 860 elite fights of generation 20. The copy is also least faithful at exactly those fights (12.6 real deaths per 100 against 10.1 in the copy; 17.5 real arrivals at the first Act 3 boss against 22.2).
4. **The game's variety is a smaller problem than the counts suggest in one place and the whole problem in two others.** The judges ask an average question (is the new fighter better where our runs die?) and 500 starts times 8 draws resolve that average to half a point whatever variety sits behind it. The average hides movement at one boss (margin about 2 points at a boss cell, 4 at a single Act 1 boss): a fighter can be promoted while getting worse at the boss that kills us. And a human line read from 32 starts swings 25 points between two draws of the same pool (Aeonglass 84.4 in the exam, 59.4 in the gate's draw; the corpus-wide rate is 72 on 1,030 arrivals): per-boss human lines must come from the corpus, not from an exam draw.
5. **What changes (D68 addendum 6):** every verdict and every quoted figure carries its margin; the whole-run judge never decides on an Act 3 figure under +1.0 per 100 runs for one candidate; a designed lane next week fills the ruler's gaps (Act 3 ordinary and elite starts and an own-arrival stratum in Acts 1-2, both from our own runs, the own starts refreshed to the live generation); human lines are quoted corpus-wide; the memory note that read "+0.53 Act 3 passes per 100 runs" as a translation is corrected; the 2 December mark stays 23 unless the owner chooses otherwise from the odds table in section 4.

## 2. Sample sizes: what a sceptic says

**The whole-run judge is blind to its own target at the step size the fight loop produces.** The judge exists to tell whether a candidate passes the first Act 3 boss more often. At 5.5 passes per 100 runs the difference of two 4,000-run arms carries an SE of 0.4 to 0.5 per 100 runs, and a fight promotion, by the one case we have, moves it by about that much. Seeing +0.5 per 100 runs 80 times in 100 would take about 25,000 pairs (40 machine-hours a candidate); +1.0 takes 6,400. So the judge decides on floors (SE 0.19 at 4,000 pairs), a proxy chosen by what is measurable. The floor proxy is not bad: v4's +0.49 floors was a real gain (2.6 SE) that missed the +0.5 minimum by one hundredth, and generation 19's +1.01 floors (5 SE) went with a real Act 3 gain (+1.43 per 100, 3.6 SE). But generation 20 was promoted on the Act 3 route at an early stop (1,100 pairs, +3.0 per 100, SE 0.8) with no floor gain at all (+0.20, SE 0.22), and the fresh reading of that step is about a third of what the stop read. The 11-21 review's decision 5 (nothing goes to the judge without an expected Act 3 gain of at least +1 per 100 runs) is the right consequence and was not followed to its end: a true +1 at 4,000 pairs is seen only about half the time, and the rule's Act 3 route can still fire on a +0.5 that read high.

**The accumulated reading is fine.** Across several promotions the gains add up (generation 18 to 20: +2.6 per 100 runs, 6 SE on 4,000 pairs). The stop rule's copy half reads exactly this and has the sample it needs.

**The weekly real block cannot see a week's progress, and the 2 December mark is a coin flip at the mark.** One block of 2,000 runs reaches the first Act 3 boss 348 times, 100 of them in the stand-in's hands. The pass rate's SE is 2.0 per 100 arrivals, the difference between two weeks' blocks 2.9. Every real-game movement quoted this month (+4.5 at the untouched bosses, +6.9 on player-finished arrivals, 11.0 to 17.5 across a referee fix) sits inside that noise, as the 11-21 review said and the week's books then forgot. The mark of 23 is 5.5 above today's 17.5, 2.7 SE: a player that did not move passes it 3 times in 1,000, which is the mark's strength; a player that truly reached 23 reads 23 or more only half the time, one at 25 four times in five. Doubling the block to 4,000 runs does not remove the coin flip at the mark (that is the nature of a point mark) but sharpens the odds around it (a true 25 passes 90 in 100, a true 20 passes 3). The wins half of the mark (10 of 2,000) behaves the same: a true 0.5 per 100 reaches 10 wins in 54 of 100 blocks.

**The fight gate is sized for the average and blind to a boss.** 500 starts times 8 draws (4,000 games a player) give the Acts 1-2 average an SE of half a point: a 2-point gain is promoted 97 times in 100, a 1-point gain 49, a no-gain candidate 2.5 per route per stage (about 6.6 across the rule). That is adequate for what the loop produces (+1.9 points). But each boss cell reads to about 2 points, one Act 1 boss (28 starts) to over 4, and the Act 3 half resolves a 2-point Act 3 gain only 64 times in 100 at stage 2. The rule's guards refuse a candidate "clearly worse" on a whole part (Acts 1-2 below -1.0, Act 3 below -2.0 at the 95 bound) and never on one boss: a fighter 4 points worse at the Test Subject and 2 better everywhere else is promoted.

**Where the samples are fine:** the human gap (66 points at the Test Subject on 80 positions: readable down to 10), the copy's stop-rule confirmation, the replay's 4-point speed effect, the cell weights for the cells that matter.

**Where no feasible sample helps:** wins. At 0.1 to 0.5 per 100 runs, telling two champions apart on wins needs tens of thousands of runs; wins must be read as the product of the stage passes, never on their own count.

**Where a sample was too small and we did not notice:** the exam's per-boss human line. 72.5, 84.4 and 65.6 (Test Subject, Aeonglass, Queen) are one human game on each of 80, 32 and 32 starts; the gate's second draw from the same pool reads 62.5, 59.4 and 62.5. The corpus holds about 1,000 human arrivals per boss (70.4, 74.1, 72.1, SE 1.4). "Ours 60.2 against the humans' 84.4 at Aeonglass" overstated that gap by about 12 points.

## 3. Representativeness

**The gate's fights against where runs die.** The ruler weights its cells by our deaths per 100 runs in the last real block, which is the right notion of "representative" for a judge whose job is to stop the dying: a fight that kills 23 of 100 runs (the Act 1 bosses) weighs ten times one that kills 2 (Act 1 hallways), and the 500 starts are spread so that each cell's spread is covered. By fight type it covers Acts 1 and 2 entirely (hallways, elites, bosses: 70 of every 100 deaths; every elite and boss encounter, 13 of the 14 Act 2 hallway encounters, 14 of the 30 Act 1 hallway encounters, which kill 2.5 runs in 100 between them) and the Act 3 bosses (18 of 100 deaths, all six boss-and-position cells). It has nothing from Act 3's ordinary and elite fights, where 12.6 of every 100 real runs die (13 in 100 of all deaths; the largest single boss, Kaiser Crab, kills 10). The reason is a choice: `rl/human_starts.py` rebuilds Acts 1 and 2 by default ("Act 3 is barely verified on the copy"), `rl/act3_starts.py` asks it for Act 3 bosses only, and `rl/act3_replay.py` keeps only boss rooms when it turns our own runs into starts; the human record holds every Act 3 floor, and our generation-20 store holds 4,115 Act 3 hallway fights across 12 encounters and 860 elite fights across 3, with the full entry state. Dropping that one filter gives the own-arrival starts; the human ones need the Acts 1-2 builder told to do Act 3. The owner's instinct ("fights throughout") is right at exactly this place, and the fix is a build, not a redesign: the weights already know the cell's size.

**Human positions against ours.** Part h is 500 human positions of 500; part a3 is 224 human openings and 190 of our own arrivals, and those 190 come from generations 17 and 18 on the old engine, not from the live champion. Position by position the humans arrive at the Acts 1-2 fights with less health than our player (median 0.64 to 0.76 of maximum against our 0.80 to 0.90), the same deck size within a card, one to three more relics, one more potion at Act 2, and the same share with Strength; at the first Act 3 boss they arrive with more health (0.94 against 0.87 to 0.92), 18 relics against our 13 to 15, and decks of the same median size but without our long tail (our 90th percentile 54 to 57 cards against 39); at the second Act 3 boss our player arrives at a median 0.27 to 0.31 of its health, humans at 0.56. From the humans' positions our fighter kills the Test Subject 6.9 times in 100, Aeonglass 60.2, Queen 59.4; from our own arrivals 3.8, 23.3 and 10.8 (lane 5, old engine). So the human positions are not easier by health; they are different by deck and relics, which is where a human run's quality sits. A fighter judged on human decks is judged on a different game from the one that kills our runs, and part h has no stratum of ours at all.

**The game's variety.** Slay the Spire 2's fights vary by enemy (30 ordinary encounters in Act 1, 14 in Act 2, 12 in Act 3; 6, 3 and 3 elites; 6, 3 and 3 bosses; the Test Subject has three forms, not two as our books say, the third reached in about 19 in 100 of its traced fights), by the deck (hundreds of cards in archetypes that fight differently), by relics, potions and health. Five hundred starts cannot sample that space and do not need to for the question the gate asks, because the gate asks about the average over where runs die and 4,000 paired games resolve that average to half a point regardless of the variety behind it. Variety bites in three other places. Per cell, as above: the game's bosses are different enough (form 2 and 3 of the Test Subject kill us with multi-hits at high Strength, kill rate 94.5 in 100 of our Test Subject fights in the store; Aeonglass and Queen are beatable) that a fighter can learn one at the cost of another and the gate cannot see the trade. In the practice: what the trainer practises is the sample that shapes the fighter, and it practises human positions plus own Act 3 boss arrivals; the Act 3 ordinary and elite fights where 13 in 100 of our deaths fall are practised only as they occur in plain runs. In the human line: a per-boss human rate on 32 starts is a different number each draw.

**The copy against the real game.** The real block is itself an unrepresentative measurement at Act 3: the referee's stand-in finished 100 of the 348 first-Act-3-boss arrivals (29 in 100), each counted as not passed, and copy figures read at about 0.75 on the real game. On the same 500 seeds the copy died in Act 3 before the first boss 7.8 times in 100 against 12.6 real, and 10.1 in the 4,000-run copy arm; it arrived at the first Act 3 boss 22.2 times in 100 against 17.5 real. The copy is least faithful in exactly the fights the ruler lacks. The 11-21 review's decision 8 (a stand-in lane) stands as the largest single risk to the 2 December reading. Smaller: the strict reader places a row by fixed boss floors (17, 33, 48) when the room string is incomplete, and about 2.5 in 100 seeds have a two-floor-longer Act 2 (boss at 35, Act 3 bosses at 50 and 51): 31 rows at floor 35 and 10 at floors 50 and 51 in the 11-20 block are placed one act off.

**The human corpus.** The live game and the corpus are the same pinned build (v0.107.1, build 23811903; `game-env/release_info.json` and the emulator's `game_version.json` agree), so no version drift. The human line is the average uploader to a public run export (14,811 Ironclad runs at Ascension 10 in the paper's copy, 18,125 in the November pull), self-selected in ways we cannot see; the export carries no player identity, so a "best players" slice cannot be cut, and none exists in our books (the only strong set ever built is the 1,608 winning runs, used once for a prior that did not help). For the questions asked this week (how far from humans at Act 3 fights) the average uploader is a lower bar than the target and the gap is 60 points, so the bar's choice changes no decision yet; it will when the gap is 10.

## 4. What changes

Orchestrator decisions (recorded as D68 addendum 6; reverse by the same route):

1. **Margins in every verdict and every quote.** The whole-run judge's verdict prints each statistic with its SE (it already carries them for floors and the deep measure; the raw Act 3 counts and wins get theirs); the gate's verdict prints each cell's difference with its margin and marks any boss cell at -3 points or worse (a flag in the record, not a refusal: at the cell margins a refusal would be noise). A figure quoted in HANDOFF, STATE, the paper or chat without its margin is a book-keeping fault. Human lines per boss are quoted from the corpus (about 1,000 arrivals a boss), never from an exam draw. Cost: Python-only, a Sonnet lane of about half an hour of machine, after the first v5 verdicts.
2. **The whole-run judge never decides on an Act 3 figure under +1.0 per 100 runs for one candidate.** Floors with the +0.5 minimum stays the decision for single candidates (it is what 4,000 pairs resolve); keep rule v3's Act 3 route keeps its +1.0 minimum and takes no early stop below 2,000 pairs (generation 20's stop read three times high). The accumulated Act 3 reading (the champion against generation 18 on 4,000 fresh pairs) is the figure that counts and is quoted beside every promotion.
3. **The ruler's gaps are filled by a designed lane next week, not this week:** Act 3 ordinary and elite starts from our own arrivals (the filter in `rl/act3_replay.py`), an own-arrival stratum in part h from the live generation's store, the 190 own Act 3 starts refreshed from the live champion at each era, all weighted by deaths like the rest; the fight exam gets the same strata as held-out cells; the human Act 3 ordinary and elite starts when the Acts 1-2 builder is told to do Act 3. A ruler change starts a new era (the incumbent replays its record, about 6.5 CPU-hours) and must not land in the week the stop rule is read. Cost: an Opus design of a page and a Sonnet build, about 4 CPU-hours.
4. **The memory note on fight-to-run translation is corrected** to "unmeasured at 4,000 pairs: +0.49 floors (2.6 SE, under the minimum by 0.01), Act 3 passes +0.53 per 100 runs (about 1 SE)". Done 2026-11-22 00:00Z.
5. **Small fixes for a free lane slot:** the strict reader reads boss floors from the seed's map length (the 2.5 in 100 longer-map seeds); the books say the Test Subject has three forms.

## 5. Rationalisations found

- "+0.53 Act 3 passes per 100 runs" and "25.5 against 23.8 per arrival" were quoted (STATE, HANDOFF, a memory note) as the v4 run candidate's whole-run translation. Both are about one SE from zero. The floor gain, which was real (2.6 SE), was quoted as "rejected" without its interval.
- "Fight-exam gain reaches runs weakly" (memory, 11-21) stated as a measurement what is an unmeasured quantity. Corrected (decision 4).
- "Ours 60.2 against the humans' 84.4 at Aeonglass" (lane 5, the paper's Act 3 table): the human side is 27 of 32 single games; the corpus rate is 72.1. The gap at Aeonglass is about 12 points, not 24.
- Generation 20 was promoted at 1,100 pairs on a +3.0 that the fresh reading put at about +1.0; the 11-21 review listed this and the week's books kept "+3.0" as the step.
- The 11-21 review said the real-game movements were within noise, and the week's books then quoted them as movements ("17.5 (11.0 before)" in STATE's lane 10 row carries no margin).
- The "wall" figures (Test Subject 1.8 in 100 arrivals on the copy; 2 of 177 at 1x in the replay) are quoted to a tenth from samples whose margin is several points.
- "The Test Subject (two forms)" in STATE and in chat: three forms.
- The gate design's "Act 3 gains of two or three points, invisible to v4, are promoted 79 and 98 times in 100" is true at stage 3; at stage 2 a 2-point Act 3 gain is promoted 64 times in 100. Not wrong, quoted at its best stage.
