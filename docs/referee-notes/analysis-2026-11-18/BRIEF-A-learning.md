## Questions

1. **What actually moved the number, generation by generation.** From `promotions.tsv`, every `agent.json` lineage and DECISIONS, build one table: generation, date, what changed (search budget, run value fit and its data, student, human prior, fight brain, data step, rule), and the gain measured at promotion (floors, Act 1 / 2 / 3 boss kills, wins per 100, n). 2. **Is the own-runs signal exhausted, and why.** Three refits of the run value on more of the champion's own runs were refused (D65 and addenda; `run-cheap/17/fits/`). Measure the variety of what the brain learns from: in `run-cheap/17/runs.jsonl` (54,000 runs) and the earlier stores, how many distinct routes (`nodes`), distinct card-pick sequences, distinct rest choices; how often two runs share the same first k decisions; the share of positions the brain has seen more than once. Compare with the human corpus's variety at the same depth (aggregates only). Say in plain terms what the brain is and is not seeing.

3. **Can this loop reach the goal at all.** The goal is to beat the best human players at Ascension 10. Put a number on the goal from the corpus aggregates (best humans' win rate and Act 3 reach at A10, cells of at least 20 runs; the exam fifth never read) and on where we are (generation 18: 0.1 wins in 100, Act 3 boss 7.9 in 100 on the copy). Then the ceilings: Monte Carlo value learning on run outcomes with a fixed search (what it can and cannot learn; the fight brain's exam ceiling of 65 against the humans' 80 in 100; the search's depth and budget, 52 CPU-seconds a run); the gap between the copy and the real game (floors 24.2 real against 29.7 copy for the last measured generation; is the loop learning the copy's quirks?). Be concrete about what each ceiling costs in Act 3 kills and wins, with the evidence or an honest "unknown".

4. **What we should do.** Options, each with cost (worker-days, machine-days), expected gain in Act 3 kills and wins per 100 (with your uncertainty), risk, and how we would know within a week: (a) variety in the data step (the plain runs forking at hard choices: second-best card, route, rest; what the literature calls exploration); (b) more search per decision (deeper or more simulations; the compute trade); (c) learning more from the human corpus (imitation or a stronger prior within D13/D38/D55); (d) a different learning target (value heads per act, win-rate at Act 3 and beyond, the owner's D66 emphasis); (e) relaxing the owner's D6/D7 constraints (no end-to-end RL, no dense rewards): say honestly what published systems that reached expert play in similar games did, and what we would give up in cost, risk and interpretability; (f) narrowing the goal (one act, one boss, a fixed deck) or stopping. Give a recommended order and say which option is the one real bet.

5. **What would change your conclusion.** The measurement or experiment, within a week on this machine, that would most reduce the uncertainty in your answer to 3 and 4.
