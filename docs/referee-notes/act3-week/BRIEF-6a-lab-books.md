## Tasks

1. **A wrapper for by-hand heavy work**: `tools/lab/labrun.py` ("lab run"): `python tools/lab/labrun.py --tag <tag> --purpose "<why>" --consumer "<who reads the result>" [--quiet] [--cores N] -- <command...>`. Runs the command at BelowNormal in a Windows Job Object (`tools/lab/winsys.py` has the pieces; the lab's `cores.py` says how the runner counts cores), records start, end, wall, CPU seconds of the whole tree, rc, tag, purpose and consumer as one JSON line in `D:/spireline/referee/LAB/hand-jobs.jsonl`, and with `--quiet` writes and removes its own `QUIET.lock` line (tag and Windows PID, the format the lab's `rl/quiet_lock.py` reads). A small reader `python tools/lab/labrun.py --report [--since <date>]` prints core-hours by tag, purpose and consumer for the runner's jobs (`LAB/runner.log` lines `rc=... cpu=...`) and the hand jobs together. Tests with a fake command.
2. **Markers carry the keep rule.** A `LAB/train/ready-*` marker may carry `keep_rule` (the rule the lane built the candidate for). The train job (`tools/lab/jobs.py`, `rl/gen0.py train`, `rl/gen_loop.py`) judges with the newer of the marker's rule and `LAB/jobs.json`'s, logs both, and the verdict (`promotion.json`, the ledger's `rule` column) records which it used and why. A marker without the field gets the lab's rule and a log line. Tests.

Tell the orchestrator the `LAB/jobs.json` change that makes the block weekly (`every_h` 168 and anything else). Tests.
