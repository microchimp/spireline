Are we doing the right things?

You asked for a sceptic's pass. Two were run on the same records: mine, and an independent reviewer that read the files fresh without my conclusions. Where it disagreed with me, I re-counted and it mostly won. The short answer: the aim is right, the measuring is now honest, the player has not moved where it counts, and this week's machine time went mostly to the wrong job.

In plain words

Right aim. Nine tenths of the distance to good human players is in the two final-act boss fights. We reach the second act's end about as often as an average human. After that we collapse: of 100 runs that reach the first final boss, we beat it 17; humans beat it 73.
No real movement. Two wins in 2,000 real games, twice in a row. The boss number went from 11 to 17.5 in 100, but most of that is the referee being fixed, not the player. Counting only the fights our player actually finished, it went from 17 to 24, which could be luck.
The machine spent its week on the wrong job. Six tenths of it played ordinary practice games whose main customer, the planner's refit, was shelved on Tuesday. The one thing aimed at the final bosses, the fighter practising those fights, got about four hours all week.
The fighter's new grader passed its first candidate for the wrong reason. The pass mark was written after that candidate's score was known, on the same games. The full-game check is the honest test: after 2,000 games each it reads about one more boss pass in 100 runs, too small to call, so it plays 2,000 more. Verdict around 9:30 pm your time.
Two of our worst-looking numbers were measured on a broken copy. The exam that said our fighter beats the Test Subject 7 times in 100 where humans manage 72, and the test that said more thinking time barely helps there, both ran before Friday's fix to how the copy handles that boss. They are being re-measured now, about three machine-hours.
The 2 December deadline had no exact pass mark. It does now, written before the data exist, below.

What changes today

  1. Idle cores practise the fights we lose. Ordinary practice games stop; the fighter's practice at final-boss fights goes from 4 processes to 12. If its learning does not move in a day, those cores were buying nothing anyway.
  2. The deadline's pass mark. On 2 December we pass if the real game shows at least 23 in 100 arrivals beating the first final boss (all arrivals counted, as today) and 4,000 fresh paired copy games show at least 2 more passes per 100 runs than the mid-November player; or at least 10 wins in 2,000 real games. Today: 17.5, about +2.4, and 2.
  3. Re-measure the Test Subject on the fixed copy before any more work is aimed at it.
  4. A better grader for the fighter: it will include final-boss fights, stop using our held-out human exam as its ruler, replay the champion's games fresh after every promotion, and demand a stricter margin. First, the promoted fighter is re-tested on fresh games; if it does not hold +0.75 points, the promotion is withdrawn.
  5. Nothing new goes to the full-game check without a stated expected gain of at least one more boss pass per 100 runs. Each check holds the whole machine for 8 to 13 hours.
  6. Honest books. Copy figures are always shown next to the real-game figure (the real game reads about three quarters of the copy right now). A claim in Friday's record that the real game "confirmed the copy" at the Test Subject is withdrawn.

The biggest remaining risk to the deadline is the referee itself: in 27 of 100 final-boss fights on the real game it loses track and a stand-in plays. Fixing that is next week's lane; its cost is unknown until designed.

3. Which loop produced nothing

  • The fight loop: 55 rejections since 9 Nov; the candidates differed from the incumbent by at most 0.01 on the old board. Rule v4's first promotion re-read the 3,200 games that motivated the rule; by design it passes a no-gain candidate about 10 times in 100. The whole-run judge at 2,000 pairs: +0.75 passes per 100 runs (SE 0.57), wins 4 against 5, extended to 4,000.
  • The planner's refit on plain runs: four refusals, parked on 18 Nov; its data supply kept running.
  • What did promote: hand-built candidates judged on whole runs. A human-in-the-loop step, not a learning loop.

5. Does each loop's output reach the player?

The champion carries fight brain v3, which took 9.2 days to get in (by a rule-timing accident). v4 reached a run candidate in forty minutes and the judge within seven hours: the wiring landed on 20 Nov works.

6. Is the Act 3 focus right?

Per 100 real runs we reach the first Act 3 boss 17.4 times and pass 3.05; humans reach it about 25 times and pass 18.2. Passing at the human rate alone would give 12.7, two thirds of the gap; arriving at the human rate alone 4.4. By stage: past the Act 2 boss 30.0 vs 28.7 (level); reaching the first Act 3 boss after it 58 vs 87 in 100; passing it 17.5 vs 72.7; winning after it 3.3 (2 of 61) vs 62. Caveats: the second boss is the largest single stage and rests on two passes; from our own arrivals our fighter wins only 4 to 23 in 100 by boss, so about four tenths of the first-boss gap is the position we arrive in, not the fight; and the Test Subject figures need the fixed engine.

8. Rationalisations found

  • The stop-rule line quoted 25.4 per 100 arrivals from a selection block; the fresh reading of the same champion is 23.3 and the real block's 17.5 is the honest figure.
  • Generation 20 was promoted at 1,100 pairs although two blocks of 2,000 had been pre-registered.
  • The minimum gain was cut from +1.0 to +0.5 the day after a near miss, against the previous day's advice; it has decided no verdict yet.
  • "17.5 (11.0 before)" was quoted as one move across a player change and a referee fix.
  • The budget ceiling moved 45 to 60 to 80 (your call). Spend at 07:00Z Saturday: 25 of 80.
