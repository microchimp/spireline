# The next run-level recipe, 2026-11-02: fit the run planner on the fight player it serves

Step C of the reset order (D44, started early by D45), part 1: the diagnosis and the build. It reads step B's boss-entry measure
(`docs/diagnostics/2026-11-02-boss-entry.md`), the recipe's code in the fork (`rl/gen_loop.py`, `rl/run_train/train.py`,
`rl/run_train/policy.py`) and the 20,049 generation 9 self-play runs on disk. No game and no emulator ran for the measurements
here (one exception, in "Unverified"). Times are Z.

## The decision

**Generation 12's run-level recipe is the fighter-matched V_run: the value head that chooses paths, card rewards, rests, shops
and events is refit only on runs played by the candidate's own fight player, with a pure Monte Carlo floor target, and the
self-play that feeds it samples its run-level choices as DESIGN 5.5 always specified (softmax over the head's values at
0.1 floors, plus 2% uniform) instead of always taking the top value.** The target stays the floor reached (the gate's measure);
the chooser stays one-step lookahead on expected floors.

- If generation 11 (step A: the loop's fight player v2 with generation 9's V_run) is promoted, generation 12 is v2 with a V_run
  fitted on about 8,000 exploring self-play runs played by generation 11. That needs about 3 to 3.5 hours of self-play, a fit of
  about 30 to 40 minutes and the usual E-block.
- If generation 11 is refused, the same recipe needs no new games: generation 9's fighter already has 20,049 runs of its own,
  so the candidate is generation 9's prior with a matched fit on those runs: a fit of about an hour (it did not fit in part 1's
  30 minutes, below) and the E-block.

## Why: four measurements on generation 9's own runs

Each self-play record keeps the value the chooser gave every option at every decision (`values`, in floors) and the floor
distribution of the option it took (`p_floor`). Generation 9's fit was trained on generations 6 to 8, so generation 9's runs are
out of sample for it. Tool: `tools/vrun_residuals.py` (about 90 seconds, one process).

1. **The head is fitted to an older, weaker fighter, and it shows.** On generation 9's runs, in Act 1, the run ends on average
   2.4 floors deeper than the head predicted for the option it took (558,852 decisions; Act 2: +0.2; Act 3: +3.0 on 5,929). Its
   own Act 1 boss-kill reading predicts a kill 38.9% of the time against 48.5% actual (746,361 rows before the boss room), a
   Brier skill of only 0.10 against the plain rate. This is the lag of a value that was learned on generations 6 to 8, whose
   fighters killed the Act 1 boss 32 to 49% of the time, and then plays for a fighter that kills it 52% (B's table 11). The
   target makes it worse: 30% of every training target is read off the network that played (the `beta` 0.3 blend of
   `train.bootstrap_targets`, live since generation 5 started writing `p_floor`), so each fit inherits part of the previous
   fighter's view. No fit so far has used the 20,049 generation 9 runs as a whole (generation 10's two fits saw 2,216 and 4,553
   of them, mixed with generations 7 and 8).
2. **Self-play never tries anything the head ranks second.** Every generation 9 record carries `tau` 0 and `eps` 0: in 759,145
   valued decisions the chosen option was the top-valued one every time. DESIGN 5.5 specifies sampling for self-play
   (`tau_g = max(0.10, 0.60 x 0.85^g)` floors, `eps` 0.02) and argmax only for evaluation; the lab's self-play plays the evaluation
   agent. So the head only ever learns about decks, routes and rests the previous head already preferred. This is how B's
   per-decision exam can find the AI passing on Shrug It Off in 11,654 of 11,654 Act 1 offers: the head has no outcome data for
   an Act 1 deck with Shrug It Off, and nothing in the recipe can ever create some. It also fits B's finding that the AI's card
   taste is the same from its own states and from the humans' (it belongs to the head, not to the states).
3. **Elites are priced by a fighter that loses to them.** Where an Act 1 map choice offers an elite beside another room type
   (39,074 choices), the head values the elite a median 2.3 floors below the best alternative (a tenth of the time more than
   6.3 below, a tenth less than 0.8 below) and takes it 1.3% of the time; in Act 2, a median 0.6 floors below, taken 8%. For
   generation 9's fighter this is probably right (B: inside its own runs, Act 1 elites do not change the boss kill, 52, 51, 51%).
   For a fighter that keeps more HP per fight, it is the first price that changes, and only a fit on that fighter's runs can
   re-price it.
4. **Act 2 choices are nearly ties, because the floor target makes them so.** In Act 2, 47% of valued decisions have the best
   and second-best options within 0.1 floors (Act 1: 18%); card rewards 63%, rests 57%, shops 70%. With a fighter that kills
   the Act 2 boss in 9% of visits and then dies early in Act 3, an Act 2 boss kill is worth only about 5 floors, so an option
   that lifts that kill from 9% to 12% is worth about 0.1 floor, the size of the noise in the head. This is why the AI does not
   build for the Act 2 boss (B: skips 8% where humans skip 34%, few potions and relics): its target, correctly for the gate,
   says it hardly matters.

## What the chooser can act on, and what B's levers need

The chooser (`run_train.policy.LookaheadPolicy`) values each option's afterstate: the run's HP, gold, deck, relics, potions,
map position and boss, plus FightNet's summary of fights against the region's encounters and the boss (the H block, the
fighter's strength as the head sees it). It decides map nodes, card rewards and skips, rest (heal or smith and which card),
shop buys and removals, reward potions and relics, events, Neow and card selections. Everything B ranks as a lever (route,
potions, relics, deck shape, rests) is one of those decisions, and every one is priced through the fighter: an elite is worth
its relic minus the HP and death risk this fighter pays; a defensive card is worth what this fighter does with it. So the
lever that moves all of B's run-level gaps at once is the fighter the head is fitted to, and step A changes the fighter.

## Alternatives, and why not now

- **A target that sees Act 2 (win chance, Act 2 boss reached or killed, conditioned on the deck).** Win chance cannot be fitted:
  0 wins in 20,049 runs. An Act 2 boss-kill head has 388 positives. "Act 2 boss reached" (22% of runs) is already readable from
  the existing 53-class floor distribution without any refit (the probability of a final floor past the Act 2 boss floor), so
  it is a change of what the chooser maximises, not of what it learns. Any such change trades mean floor for deeper runs, and the
  gate's primary is mean floor: moving the Act 2 boss kill from 9% to 20% of the 22% of runs that reach it adds about 0.12
  floors, a quarter of the gate's resolution (0.5). It would need a new promotion measure (an owner decision) and a fighter that
  wins Act 2 boss fights; B puts 27 to 44 of the 67-point Act 2 gap in the fight itself. Revisit when the Act 2 boss kill share
  reaches about 25%; then the floor target itself starts to value it.
- **Human data (the four training fifths, D38).** Three problems. D38 allows the four fifths as fight-gym training starts; using
  run outcomes as labels for the run head is the label use D13 rules out and D38 did not reopen, so it needs the owner. The
  outcomes are a human fighter's: B's AI-fitted model rates the humans' Act 1 hands 7 points below the AI's own for the AI's
  fighter, so a head learned from human outcomes would steer the AI toward decks it plays worse. And the corpus has no map or
  room order and unchecked potions, while the head's input includes the map position (B's exam needed four borrowed maps per
  state). The exam fifth stays the yardstick for the new head (B's per-decision exam, about 12 minutes).
- **Refit on more of the same, or ensembles of fits.** Generation 10's two refits (new lab prior plus a refit on generations 7
  to 9) gave +0.2 and -1.4; more data from the same greedy policy cannot show the head an option it never takes. An ensemble
  reduces noise between near-ties, which item 4 shows are mostly worth under 0.1 floor. Not first.
- **A hand-made bonus for potions, relics or boss damage.** D6 rules out hand-shaped rewards.

## Expected gain and cost

The gate resolves about 0.5 floors (paired SE about 0.2 on 2,000 seeds). Floors at the margin: an extra Act 1 boss kill is worth
about 13 floors (from 17 to the 30 that generation 9's Act 2 entrants average), so 1 point of Act 1 boss kill is about 0.12
floors; an Act 2 boss kill about 5.

- **With v2 (generation 12):** +0.8 floors expected (range 0 to +2), about +3 points of runs entering Act 2 and +1 of runs
  reaching the Act 2 boss, on top of what generation 11 brings. Basis: each refit that followed a fighter change gained +0.75 to
  +2.0 (generations 6 to 9), and v2 is a larger change of fighter than any of them (+10 points at the Act 1 boss in the loop's
  replays); the stale-fit penalty measured in item 1 is what the refit removes. Exploration adds little to the first fit's
  floors; it is there so the next fits can learn the options the head has never tried. Cost on the machine: about 3 to 3.5
  hours of self-play (8,000 runs; generation 9's blocks ran about 2,600 runs an hour on 16 processes; exploration at 0.1 floors
  costs about 1.1 floors a run of self-play by the head's own values: 13% of Act 1 and 33% of Act 2 picks are not the top
  value), about 30 to 40 minutes of fit (one core reads the shards, then the card), and one E-block (2 to 3 hours).
- **Without v2 (generation 11 from generation 9's runs):** +0.3 floors expected (range -0.5 to +1.0): the fit removes the lag
  of item 1 but not the missing exploration. Cost: the fit (about an hour, no games) and the E-block.

## What would make me switch

- To the cheaper test, **v2 with the generation 9 matched fit** (no self-play, an E-block only): if the machine cannot give 3.5
  hours of self-play before the night. It spends one of generation 12's two attempts on a fit made for another fighter, so it is
  second choice.
- Away from matching: if the matched fit's out-of-fold Act 1 skill on generation 9's runs is not clearly above the 0.10 that
  generation 9's own head scores on them (item 1), the lag is not the problem and the next lever is the exploration alone.
- To an Act 2 target: once the Act 2 boss kill share passes about 25% (above), or the owner sets a promotion measure that counts
  acts cleared.

## The build (fork worktree `D:/spireline/fork/wt-reset-c`, branch `reset-c`, from fork main `88908c24`)

- `rl/gen_loop.py`: `fighter_manifests` (the self-play blocks one prior played, from each manifest's agent block);
  `fit_run_value_matched` (FightNet, the value head and the D33 keep record on that window, `beta` 0, every generation of the
  window in V's buffer, the train job's check that no E-block seed is in the window, `vrun_fit.json` beside the fit);
  `read_vrun_fit` (only a kept fit whose file matches its record is read); `train_run_value` takes `value_generations` and
  `beta` (defaults unchanged, so the train job's recipe is untouched); `build_candidate(vrun_fit=...)` puts such a fit into a
  built candidate (step A's path) and records whether its runs were played by the candidate's prior; `exploring_agent` and
  `Agent.describe` naming `tau` and `eps` when they are not zero (argmax agents describe themselves as before, so arm identities
  and resumes are unchanged).
- `rl/gen0.py`: `emu --explore-tau T --explore-eps E` (refused without `--record`, so E-blocks, nights and scores always play
  the argmax agent); `vrun-fit` (BelowNormal priority); `build --vrun-fit DIR`.
- Tests: `tests/python/test_vrun_matched.py` (14). The fork's `test_gen_loop.py` and `test_gen0.py` pass (80). The commit is `5448a451` on branch `reset-c`.

The part 2 run, in order, once A has landed and the machine is free (commands use the lab's paths):

1. Generation 11 promoted: self-play under it, recorded and exploring, without the lab's train marker (so the train job does
   not train an ordinary candidate from these shards): `gen0.py emu --stream <job> --config a3-v1 --procs 16 --agent-root
   <AGENT> --record --explore-tau 0.1 --explore-eps 0.02 --out <job> --prior-server auto ...` for about 8,000 runs.
2. `gen0.py vrun-fit --shards-root <LAB>/runs --gen-dir <AGENT>/gen11/candidate --gens 11 --out <fit> --lab <LAB>
   --device cuda` (30 to 40 minutes; exit 3 if the fit fails the keep test, and then nothing is built).
3. `gen0.py build --prior <AGENT>/gen11/candidate --base <AGENT>/gen11/candidate --vrun-fit <fit> --candidate-generation 12
   --why "D45 step C: fighter-matched V_run" --marker-dir <LAB>/train`; the train job judges it on the promotion path as it
   judges any built candidate.
4. Generation 11 refused: step 3 with `--prior` and `--base` generation 9 and `--vrun-fit` a fit made with `vrun-fit --gens 9` (about an hour); the candidate's
   number is the orchestrator's call (generation 11's attempts are counted per number).

Checks before the E-block, both cheap: B's per-decision exam with the new head (12 minutes; expect more skips in Act 2, more
Act 1 elites taken) and `tools/vrun_residuals.py` on the new runs.

## The part 1 fit: started, stopped at the time limit

The fit on the 20,049 generation 9 runs (`gen0.py vrun-fit --prior-sha a12ee8e8... --gens 9`, GPU venv, BelowNormal, 4
threads) started at 02:56Z. It was stopped at 03:23Z, at part 1's 30-minute limit. It was still in its first phase, one core
reading and encoding the shards (1,850 CPU-seconds, 3.8 GB, nothing written). The full fit therefore needs about 45 to 60
minutes when the machine is free. It is the first item of part 2 in the refused branch, and in the promoted branch it is the
cheap check of the switch test above (its out-of-fold Act 1 skill against the 0.10 of item 1). The code path (window, keep
record, `vrun_fit.json`, the build) is covered by the unit tests with a stand-in trainer. It has not yet run end to end on real
shards.

## Unverified

- The expected gains are judgement from the generation history and the measurements above, not a test; the E-block decides.
- The exploration settings are DESIGN 5.5's floor values; their cost was estimated from the head's own values, not played.
- Three related fork test modules (`test_prior_plumbing`, `test_run_train_policy`, `test_run_train_selfplay`) showed 2 failures
  of 90 in one run; they play small emulator runs, so they were not rerun (part 1 does not play games) and the failures are not
  yet attributed. That one run played two 6-simulation emulator runs in a test folder, about 4 seconds; nothing of the lab was
  touched. Part 2 runs the fork's gate before landing.
- The Act 1 skill comparison uses the playing net's recorded readings against a pooled rate, the keep test uses fold fits
  against the training rate; close, not identical.
