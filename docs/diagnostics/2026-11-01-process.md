# Process diagnostic, 2026-11-01: what is wrong, top to bottom

Owner's request in chat, 2026-11-01 21:14Z: "run a diagnostic to see what is wrong with the process top to bottom".
The facts are a read-only worker's sheet, [2026-11-01-facts.md](2026-11-01-facts.md) (about 1 point of credit; every
number carries its source). This file is the orchestrator's reading of that sheet. The goal is to beat the best human
players at Ascension 10, Ironclad first. Times are Z.

## The answer in one paragraph

The process measures the wrong thing, trains two brains that cannot reach the exam, and spends its clock on bookkeeping.
The exam says generation 9 reaches the Act 1 boss more often than the average human (92% of runs against 74%) and loses
to it more often (kills it in 52% of visits against 74%), then dies in Act 2 (Act 2 boss killed in 2.2% of runs against
29.1%); 0 wins in 8,104 real runs since 10-26. The AI's hallway and elite fights are not the problem; the deck and
health it brings to bosses are, and nothing in the process measures that or trains for it directly. The one measured
improvement in hand, the always-on loop's fight player v2 (+10 points over the exam's player on Act 1 boss starts), is
wired to nothing. The run-level recipe has been the same since generation 5 and its gains have fallen to zero; no next
recipe exists and its practice is paused. Both trainers are starved of data while 44% of the machine's core-hours and
about 78% of the GPU sit unused, and 58% of the lab's clock goes to fidelity bookkeeping whose output no longer changes,
on a copy that already matches the real game in mean.

## Findings, ranked by cost to the goal

1. **No instrument on what decides the game.** Where gen 9's 493 runs of 11-01 ended: 8% before the Act 1 boss, 44% at
   it, 28% in Act 2's floors, 18% at the Act 2 boss, 2% in Act 3. The AI reaches the Act 1 boss in 92% of runs (humans
   74%) and kills it in 52% of visits (humans 74%); given Act 1 cleared, humans kill the Act 2 boss in 53% of runs, the
   AI in 4.6%. So the AI arrives at bosses with a weaker deck or less health than humans do. The process has no
   measure of deck, relics, upgrades or HP at boss entry, for the AI or against humans (the corpus has them: 55% of
   rests upgrade, 9.2 cards taken a run, 54 of 81 HP at the boss). The per-decision human exam decided in D13 and D38
   (a fixed held-out fifth of the corpus) was never built. No best-human line exists, only the average player's. The
   headline, mean floors at ±0.7, is a coin flip on the Act 1 boss and cannot see Act 3 or wins (11 Act 2 clears and 0
   wins a night). Confidence high on the counts.
2. **The improvement in hand is wired to nothing.** On the fight board's 200 Act 1 boss starts the exam's player a3-v1
   wins 36.9%, judge-v0 44.1%, the loop's v1 45.7% and v2 47.0%. The nightly agent still plays a3-v1 with the lab's
   prior; there is no path from the loop's players to `rl/nightly_real.py`, `gen_loop.py` or `LAB/jobs.json`. The loop
   cost about 20 credit points on 10-30 and takes about 5 cores for 18 hours a day; the lead "loop v2 into the run-level
   agent" has been open since 10-30 without a brief. The process built a trainer before its consumer, and whether
   boss-start skill transfers to whole runs is unmeasured. Confidence high on the facts, the transfer is open.
3. **The run-level recipe is saturated and idle.** Map, rest, shop and card choices come from V_run, a value head refit
   each generation on 8-10k self-play runs with a floor-reached target, the same recipe since generation 5 (D33). Paired
   floor gain per generation on the lab's 2,000-seed block: +5.0 (gen 5, the recipe change), +1.7, +0.75, +2.0, +1.0,
   then generation 10's two attempts +0.2 and -1.4, both refused. Two attempts of one recipe differ by 1.7 floors while
   the gate can resolve 0.5 (SE 0.2, lower bound above 0): the gate is sound, the recipe makes less than it can see.
   No generation 11 recipe exists; self-play is paused (D43). The lab's fight prior is distilled from a3-v1's own
   search, a weaker teacher than the loop's player. Confidence high on the history; the cause of saturation (thin Act 2
   data in a floor target, the teacher, the data cap) is inference.
4. **Both trainers are data-starved on an idle machine.** The lab's train job draws 6,400 of 75-120k available fights
   per candidate (5-13%). The loop's trainer has used 87 seconds of GPU in 31 hours and waited 163,000 seconds for
   fresh practice; its practice set is 1,478 starts from 200 runs of 4 nights (168 of them Act 1 boss starts); its
   held-out loss is 1.0721 against v2's 1.0776 and top-1 agreement 0.7388 against 0.7402, so its candidates are v2

5. **The lab clock goes to bookkeeping that does not change.** Lab jobs were booked for 137 of 168 hours but used 25% of

   was identical in 133 of 142 consecutive runs; the groups file changes by a median of 1 group in 4,867 per run. The
   presort reruns the codex and corpus checks on every landing (23 runs, 23.4 hours). Fidelity-side jobs take 58% of

6. **Fidelity no longer limits the score.** Same seeds, generation 9's three nights, copy against real: floors 22.48
   against 22.12, 22.30 against 22.53, 22.63 against 22.70; Act 1 boss kill 50-51% against 51-54%; both arms die in

## What the process should do instead: the reset order (2026-11-03 21:00Z), recorded as D44

Ordered by expected floors per credit point. Nothing starts before the reset (usage 67-68%, ceiling 70) except E on the
owner's word.

- **A. Wire the loop's fight player into the exam agent** as generation 11, a real change: the lab's 2,000-seed block
  and then the nightly play with the loop's v2 (judge-v0 leaves where the coverage census allows), measured by the
  promotion guard and one night. Opus, about 2 points; machine one block (about 1.5 hours) and one night. Expected: Act
  1 boss kills from 52% toward 60% and more Act 2 entries; the first real-game test of the fight player. Rule from
  finding 2: no trainer is built or kept without its consumer wired first.
- **B. The boss-entry instrument and the human per-decision exam.** For every nightly run: deck, relics, upgrades and
  HP at Act 1 and Act 2 boss entry, card-pick, rest and path rates, against the corpus aggregates and the D38 held-out
  fifth. Sonnet, about 1.5 points. This names which run-level choices differ from humans; the next recipe is built from
  it, not guessed.
- **C. The next run-level recipe, from B.** Candidates: a V_run target that can see Act 2 (win chance conditioned on
  the deck, D37's run judge), training on the stronger fight player's runs, human data per D38. One Opus diagnosis, then
  a Sonnet build, about 3 points; self-play re-enabled when it starts.
- **D. The lab clock.** Skip the groups, kits, codex and corpus steps when their inputs' hashes are unchanged, and run
  bookkeeping beside replays instead of after them. Sonnet, about 1 point; frees about 30% of the lab's clock and the
  cores for A-C.
- **E. The loop's engine move** (0.5 point, 30 machine-minutes), then, only if A confirms transfer, feed it (D38's
  diverse starts) and raise the lab's fight cap. If A shows no transfer, the loop stops and its 5 cores a day go to C.
- **F. The ledger.** Every worker's start and end usage reading goes in its HANDOFF line (no cost); the paper stays at
  one edition a day plus a line per landing.

Not changed: the exam's size (500 runs; floors at ±0.7; Act 2 rates are read from the emulator's 2,000-seed block, since
the arms agree in mean), the lab gate's statistics, the night driver.
